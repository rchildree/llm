# LLM — Latin Learning Machine

One site for Latin students: **vocab flashcards**, **form-identification
drills** ("which of these is accusative plural?") and **agreement practice**,
built on a pre-generated dataset (731 words → ~28,000 surface forms with full
grammatical analyses).

No accounts, no server, no progress tracking. Flashcards are a shuffled pass
through the selected vocabulary — every word once per pass, a random direction
each card, and never the same word twice in a row — flipped to reveal, or
answered from four options with the **Multiple choice** switch. Both pages share
one chapter setting: *through* chapter N, or **only ch. N** for that chapter
alone.

The **Agreement** section holds four exercises. *Does it match?* judges whether
two inflected forms can stand together; *Make it agree* inflects a modifier and
its noun into one named case and number by typing the endings; *Fill in the
chart* types out both paradigms in parallel, ten rows deep, from one worked
example; and *Gender* asks a noun's gender and then explains the answer with
the rules that bear on that word, and only those — the declension defaults, the
third-declension ending patterns (-tiō, -tās, -men, -us/-oris and the rest) and
the natural genders that override them. Nouns obey the chapter setting, but
modifiers are always drawn from everything through the cap: applying *only
ch. N* to both would leave 16 of the 45 chapters with nothing to practise on.
